# The balance run for launch, 2026-10-23

LAUNCH.md workstream #7, his ruling in GDD §18 (2026-10-23): a balance run
sets the economy and pacing values against §6.3's targets, and he approves
its summary before they count. Every value changed below keeps its
`PLACEHOLDER` marker until his yes. Run on commit ae477e5's code; the model
is `tools/balance_model.py`, extended for the systems the game gained since
the last run (see "The model"). Nothing was run in Studio. The run was made
on Opus (commit a342f3a) and reviewed by Fable, the model he chose for it,
the same night: what the review changed is marked **Fable's review** below
and is in the numbers of this file.

## The values, one by one

Each: file, old → new, the reason, with the what-ifs that chose it (all run
in memory with `--set`; the sweeps are in the session, the finals below).

1. **`Constants.Economy.LocationPayMultiplier` 20 → 5.** With ×20 the second
   hotel's first bus paid 20× the Manor's, so Frostfang Lodge reached 5★ in
   5–9 minutes and Bayou Spa in 6–11, and the chain's whole time was saving
   for the next price (`before_*`: Frostfang's leg 13–19 min against 60–90).
   The pieces' rows keep their ×20 a location (not touched: they are his
   prices by kind), so a Frostfang room now costs 4× the minutes of income
   of a Manor room and a Bayou room 16×; that is where "each next location
   dearer" lives now, and §6.2's minutes per purchase rise with the location
   (1.0 / 0.8 / 1.3 for the efficient player). Swept 20, 12, 10, 8, 7, 6, 5:
   at 8 and above Bayou Spa's leg came in under 90 min for the efficient
   player however the prices were set; at 5 all three players land in the
   bands with the prices below. The ×20 route kept for comparison: opening
   prices $800k / $30M give 41 / +1:22 / +2:31 / 4:32 (efficient), 54 /
   +1:25 / +2:13 / 4:38 (casual), 38 / +1:01 / +1:34 / 3:13 (payer), with a
   1:28 gap at Frostfang and Bayou Spa a 10-minute hotel.
2. **`Constants.Chain.OpenCost` {250000, 10000000} → {400000, 1000000}.**
   With ×5 pay the $10M is unreachable in a session; swept $300k–$550k for
   Frostfang and $350k–$1M for Bayou. $400k puts Frostfang's saving at
   23–35 min at the Manor's $10–20k a minute. The first run set Bayou at
   $550k (its saving 21–41 min at Frostfang's $20–40k); **Fable's review**
   raised it to $1M so his ruling "each exponentially dearer" (§6.4) holds
   in the prices themselves, not only in the rooms: measured against $550k
   and $800k (8 seeds, three players), $1M costs the efficient player 3
   minutes on Bayou's leg (2:03 against 1:50, now 4 over the band), the
   payer 7 (1:56) and the casual player 5 (1:30), because Bayou's time is
   its rooms (a Bayou Standard Room's key piece is ×400 the Manor's at pay
   ×25), not its price. The Swedish summary carries the trade.
3. **`Constants.Rooms.ComfortPerRoom` 12 → 2.5** (cap 60 unchanged). With
   12, five furnished rooms filled Comfort and 5★ came at 23–26 min with a
   10-room hotel; the bars' satisfaction already makes every review 5. Swept
   6, 4, 3, 2.5, 2, 1.5: 2.5 gives 41 min (efficient) and 55 (casual); 2
   gave 32–39 for the efficient player because the hotel then builds for
   Comfort longer; 1.5 gave 45 but Frostfang 1:00+ and a 46-minute hotel
   there. 2.5 means a hotel of ~24 full-bar rooms reaches the cap, and a
   5★ hotel has 12–20 rooms at every location.
4. **`Constants.Contracts.RewardMinutes` 15 → 4** (× the rarity factor 1–6,
   unchanged). At 15 the three contracts a day paid $310k–$460k in a run,
   a Frostfang opening on their own; at 4 they pay $120k–$150k (6 % of run
   cash for the efficient player; the casual player, who plays over five
   days and does 12, 32 % of it together with the dailies and the offline
   till). Swept 15, 5, 4, 0.
5. **`DailyService` `REWARD_MINUTES` 8 → 4.** Three a day at 8 min paid
   $4.0M of a casual player's $7.6M run at the old values. Swept 8, 4.
6. **The reward floor BasePay × 10 → × 2, now `Constants.Economy.RewardFloor`.**
   The floor under every "minutes of income" reward was $600/min while the
   first room earns $60/min: the first login (3 min) paid $1,800 and the
   first daily $4,800 at minute 4, when cash is $100–$300. At × 2 they pay
   $360 and $480 (the model: `login` $360). Swept 10, 2. **Fable's review:**
   the first run changed the literal in `StoreService.incomeRate` only;
   `ContractService.baseRate` (the VIP reward's income, the VIP's own pay
   taken out) had its own `BasePay * 10`, so the VIP floor was still $600 a
   minute. One value in `Constants` now, read by both; the model reads it
   and guards both lines.
7. **The codes.** `WELCOME` $500 kept (about 5 minutes of a new hotel's
   income at minute 5). `SPOOKY` 3 → 5 shards: a Moon Tree node's first level
   costs 5 (`MoonTree.luau`, growth ×1.5), so 3 bought nothing. `HALLOWEEN`
   added (his ruling 2026-10-23): $1,000 and 5 shards, the season's code a
   little bigger than the other two. Candy would suit it better but codes
   grant cash and shards only, and the freeze rules out a new field.

Checked and left: the bus (3–8, every 3 min, his go), the stays (doubled,

## The model

`tools/balance_model.py` reads every number from `src/` (a Luau-literal
parser; 120 guard lines stop it when a formula it mirrors changes shape) and
simulates one player's first run of the chain second by second. This run
added what the game gained since `BALANCE-2026-10-21b.md`, each read from
the code:

- **The lumps of "minutes of income"** and their floor: the login calendar,
  the three daily requests (drawn as `DailyService.draw` does, a reroll
  for a request the player cannot do), Hunter Night's reward, the VIP
  contracts' reward (`Contracts.reward`), the Starter Pack.
- **VIP contracts** as `Contracts.spec` and `ContractService` run them: the
  ledger from 2★, offers by `RarityByStars` and `RarityWeights`, a bar above
  the best room's, a second from 3.5★, the clock as play time at the
  contract's own hotel, the room held `Reserved`, the VIP an extra seat on
  the next bus at `VipPay` ×, the reward on a happy stay without the VIP's
  own pay, three a day. The player builds or fills for a contract when it
  costs ≤ 15 minutes of income, else cancels it (a policy assumption).
- **Resident levels** (XP a minute at work, the price from
  `ResidentLevels.price`, +10 % Wing / +3 % staff a level).
- **Three players** (`--player`): the *efficient* one every earlier run
  modelled; the *casual* one plays 45 minutes a day (slower at the desk and
  the mop, collects every 2 minutes, answers 3 requests in 10, looks at the
  shop every 20 s; the guests leave with the server, the residents' offline
  till pays to the cap, a bus at a random phase the next day, the UTC day
  turns); the *payer* owns Auto-Collect, Resident Wing+, Extra Floor, VIP
  Lobby and Night Manager (`payer+starter` also the Starter Pack).
- The 2x Cash boost on guests and the Wing; `--season halloween`; `--codes`;
  `--no-extras` for the model as it was.

Two bugs in the extension were found and fixed in the run (a contract
asking for Size and Decor built a new room every look; the daily "Collect"
stuck for the Auto-Collect payer); the casual player's zero-5★ result that
showed the first was a model fault, not a balance one.

### What it still leaves out (read the numbers with these in mind)

- **Other players.** The bus is split between the hotels on a street
  (`VacantShare` of the street's vacancy, `MinPerPlayer`–`MaxPerPlayer`
  per player), guests browse and choose by rating and preferences, pitching,
  likes and the friend bonus (+10 %): the run is one player alone on each
  street. With company, a new hotel at a location gets fewer of the bus's
  guests than the model gives it, so the later hotels' "in the hotel" time
  is a floor, not a ceiling.
- **Travelling back** to a hotel left, the time a Travel takes, and the
  background rate of a hotel left below 5★ (the model leaves at 5★ only).
- **Expeditions** (not built), the Moon Tree and Prestige Stars (none in a
  first run), Book family bonuses, the Full Moon weekend, the Monster Parade,
  the Candy shop, Winter.
- **Finds** (the 2 % roll, the pity, the set bonus to a room's Decor bar),
  souvenirs, found pieces in the shop, residents' perks.
- **The other staff roles** (Front Desk, Housekeeping, Bellhop,
  Entertainment, Security): one Room Service per hotel is modelled; the
  rating's Front Desk and Housekeeping coverage points (10 + 10) are never
  earned, so 5★ is a little harder in the model than in the game.
- **Room shapes and packing**: cells are counted, walls priced at 75 % of a
  compact rectangle's perimeter, furniture and decor on at most half the
  squares; nothing is routed. Rooms are never enlarged, moved, retyped or
  sold; hallway decor, finishes and windows are not bought.
- **The policy is a policy**: one greedy buyer, stated in the file's header.
  A real player's order differs; the casual profile's numbers (slower at
  everything, buying every 20 s) are an assumption, not a measurement.
- **Nothing was run in Studio.** The first in-game check is the launch run
  (LAUNCH.md #11): the first 15 minutes against §6.3's early rows, which
  the run did not change.

## Fable's review (the same night, as the model he chose for the run)

Read with fresh eyes: the extended model, the seven values and their
reasons, the blind spots. Agreed with the method (one model reading the
code, three players, the lumps of income modelled) and with five of the
seven values as they stood; changed two, and one comment:

1. **Bayou Spa's opening price $550k → $1M** (value 2 above): the first run
   let the prices go flat ($400k → $550k) and put "dearer" in the rooms
   alone; his ruling says the hotels themselves are exponentially dearer,
   and the price lever is nearly free (3 minutes). Measured, not guessed.
2. **The reward floor was changed in one of two places** (value 6): the VIP
   contracts kept the × 10 floor. Now one PLACEHOLDER value,
   `Economy.RewardFloor`, in `Constants`, where every value lives.
3. `Finds.SouvenirCost`'s comment said "×20 a location, as pay": the
   souvenirs follow the pieces' rows (×20), not the pay (×5 now). Comment
   only; the values stand.

Looked at and left: ×5 against ×20 (the ×20 route leaves Bayou a 10-minute
hotel, measured; the GDD's ×20 is a starting value, and the rows' ×20 keep
every later piece dearer in income); Comfort 2.5 (the one category that
needs a hotel, not a furnished room; 5★ arrives with Comfort 63–81, the
rounding carrying it); the VIP and daily minutes (a day's lumps are now 5–6 %
of an efficient run, 33 % of a casual one over five days with the offline
till, which is the return hook working); the codes.

Does the model cover what drives pacing now? Built rooms, the chain's
background earnings, VIP contracts, resident levels, the size-and-look
mutation stack and the way to rebirth-ready: yes, each read from the code.
Not covered, and worth saying plainly: **the run after a rebirth** (§6.3's
last row, "faster"): residents kept from Bayou earn ×25 in the Wing from the
Manor's first minute (six of them about $2,000 a minute against the first
room's $60), so the second run's Manor is a formality; that is the design
("the head start"), but its length is unmeasured. **Finds** are not modelled
beyond the 2026-10-21c check. **Other players** are the biggest unknown: a
street with four hotels splits one bus four ways, and every "in the hotel"
time above is a floor.
