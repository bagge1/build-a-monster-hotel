# Economy pacing model, 2026-10-19

These are measurements, not tuning. No value in the game was changed. The model is
`tools/balance_model.py`. It reads every number from `src/shared/*.luau` and
stops if a formula it copies has changed in the code. It simulates one solo
player's first run at Location 1, second by second, using 8 seeds per scenario,
and every figure below is the median across seeds. Command:
`python tools/balance_model.py --seeds 8 --hours 8`. The docstring of
`balance_model.py` lists every assumption and the buying policy.

**The player assumed.** Plays without stopping and is quick: checks a guest in
4 s after it reaches the desk, cleans a room 5 s after check-out, collects the
till every 30 s, and handles 60 % of requests in person. The first resident goes
on Room Service. The greedy buying policy buys: decor that matches its guests
when it costs up to 90 s of income; up to 3 amenities per floor; the first room
of each new type; then whichever new room or tier upgrade gives the most extra
income per $ spent. Space is a budget of about 80 to 84 cells per floor, which
is about 20 rooms of 2×2. Not modelled: Hunter Night, events, daily rewards,
passes, the Moon Tree, offline income, resident perks and pitching.

## Findings (the values are August's to set; options only)

1. **The tutorial's Vampire Count comes about 12 minutes earlier than the target,
   and moves in after its first stay.**
   - `TutorialService` sends the Vampire as soon as the steps after MoveIn
     close: Goal 8 s, Clean, Staff, and Preview at most 25 s. That puts it at
     about 2:30, against the 10–15 minutes of the target.
   - `ResidentService` gives every guest whose source is `Tutorial` the
     friendship threshold `Residents.TutorialFriendship` (1). The scheduled
     Vampire is such a guest, and it is also forced happy by `happyFloor`.
   - So the rebirth's named-monster requirement (§6.4) is met at 7:38, as a
     gift.
   - Options: give the threshold of 1 only to the tutorial Slime, so the Vampire
     needs Legendary friendship. Separately, gate the Goal card or the Vampire on
     time, for example the first :00/:30 bus after 10:00.
2. **Without the tutorial, the Vampire Count resident is the slowest of the three
   rebirth requirements: about 4 hours, against 60–90 minutes.**
   - It needs `Rarity.Legendary.friendship` = 10 happy stays. A Vampire arrives
     only on :00/:30 buses (`Bus.LegendaryEvery` 1800) or at `weight` 1 out of
     100 per bus seat. That is about 2.5–3 Vampires an hour, and
     `moveInChance` adds only 3 % per stay.
   - The first Vampire stay comes at about 35 minutes, because the Coffin Suite
     needs 3.5★ and $40k (A.2) while income is about $1–4k a minute.
   - With other players on the street it gets slower: each Vampire goes to only
     one hotel.
   - Options: a lower threshold for the location's rebirth monster, more
     scheduled Legendary appearances, or friendship from more than happy stays.
3. **The rating is easy to raise.** Without the tutorial it reaches 4★ at about
   17 minutes and 5★ at about 45. Three things cause it:
   - Reviews make up half the rating (A.4), and almost every stay reviews 5★
     once a floor has 3 amenities. Satisfaction is 50 + 2×15 for preferences +
     15 for amenities + 10 for a request = 105, which clamps to 100, and
     `1 + floor(sat/25)` gives 5★ (`Stay.SatisfactionAmenityMax`,
     `SatisfactionPerAmenity`, A.3).
   - Fun and Style fill up cheaply. `Rating.FunPerAmenity` 15 counts every
     amenity in the hotel. `StylePerDecor` 4 reaches 100 with about 25 pieces of
     $30 decor.
   - With no reviews yet, one 4★ review lifts the rating from 1.5 to 2–2.5★.
     That unlocks the Sarcophagus Suite at 0:35 and the Potion Loft and Den Room
     by about 2:10 (Pieces `unlock`).
   - Options: count reviews only after N stays, or weight them less. Make a 5★
     review harder to get. Base Fun and Style on distinct pieces or on a
     per-room figure.
4. **Income stops growing after about 30–45 minutes, at roughly $20–26k a
   minute.**
   - `WalkIn.MinInterval` (8 s) caps walk-ins at 7.5 a minute. The formula
     reaches that cap at about 20 rooms at 5★.
   - Three floors (`Economy.FloorCap`, about 48 rooms) are full by 20–45
     minutes.
   - After that only tiers raise income. A tier costs `TierCostMultiplier` 4
     times the previous one and adds `TierPayBonus` +50 % pay. A T5 Sarcophagus
     Suite costs $384k for about +$1.5/s × milestone.
   - $1M still arrives inside the band, at 62 and 79 minutes. On the tutorial
     path, though, the last 40 minutes before rebirth add nothing new to the
     hotel.
   - Options: a lower walk-in floor or more bus guests, so that more rooms add
     income; or a gentler late tier curve.
5. **Purchases cost far less than §6.2's 2–5 minutes of income, and milestones
   pay for empty rooms.**
   - `RoomCostGrowth` 1.15 counts only rooms of the same type, from a $50 base.
     Decor costs $30–300. Meanwhile milestones multiply income: ×2 at 5, 10 and
     25 rooms of a type (`Economy.Milestones`).
   - Milestones count rooms owned, not rooms used. The greedy player owns about
     20 Standard Rooms, and about 10 of them are always empty. Building empty
     rooms to reach a milestone is the best buy.
   - The gaps longer than 10 minutes come from saving for a few large prices:
     the $40k Coffin Suite at minutes 10–27, and later T5 tiers ($100k–640k).
   - Options: price growth over all rooms owned, or a steeper growth rate.
     Count a milestone on occupied or upgraded rooms. Fill the gap between $1.5k
     and $40k with prices in between.
6. **Two small misses without the tutorial.**
   - The first guest pays at 1:13. The walk-in timer is set when the player
     joins, while the hotel has 0 rooms, so it waits `WalkIn.MaxInterval`
     (60 s).
   - The first resident moves in at 7:11. It needs 2 happy stays (Common
     `friendship`), and a happy stay needs both preferences matched in decor.

**With several players** (not simulated): walk-ins are private, so the cap in
finding 4 still holds. Bus guests are split by `Scoring`, where rating^1.5
dominates. The bus grows by only 1 per player, so a busy street gives each
hotel fewer bus guests. Rare guests go to one hotel each, which makes finding 2
k times slower for k competing 5★ hotels, unless pitching wins them. The
newcomer boost matters only below 2★, which a new hotel leaves within minutes
(finding 3).

**The assumptions that move these numbers most:** the player is always present
and quick; the space budget per floor sets the plateau in finding 4; the walk-in
picks a species uniformly from those with a vacant room, as the code does; the
greedy policy is one decent player, not the best possible play; and Hunter
Night (+5 minutes of income every 90 minutes) and resident perks are left out.
